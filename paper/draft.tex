\begin{abstract}

Modern data curation pipelines combine rules, similarity search, deduplication, and learned quality scores, but often make it difficult to trace why an individual example was kept, removed, quarantined, or restored. We introduce evidence-bound curation, a framework that separates evidence from authority: each curation signal emits inspectable evidence, including pass/fail/abstain judgments where applicable, and an explicit policy determines which evidence may authorize which action on the corpus. Rather than collapsing Validity, Redundancy, Quality, and Coverage into interchangeable keep/drop votes, the framework gives each an asymmetric decision role. Validity failures isolate an item directly. Redundancy may remove an item only when a supporting relation is verified and a representative is retained. Quality is assessed by a frozen four-head ranker whose heads return pass, fail, or abstain. The policy admits an item only when semantic coherence, substantive payload, and recoverable learnable relations provide positive support; correctness acts solely as a qualified-failure veto. Coverage may restore previously discarded items but can never initiate a deletion. Every accepted action records a content fingerprint, policy version, decision reason, and, where applicable, a representative or restoration link, making the corpus reproducible and auditable at the level of individual decisions. We instantiate one frozen policy on code and mathematics corpora and evaluate it by continued pretraining on all retained packed tokens, against no-update and Raw controls and same-corpus Data-Juicer and NeMo Curator recipes under a common protocol. Retaining 89.4\% of code and 82.6\% of math packed tokens, the frozen policy outperformed both curation baselines on three of six code benchmarks but trailed both baselines on all four math benchmarks. Auditable, non-collapsed decision rules can therefore produce reproducible corpora with per-decision provenance, while downstream effects differed between the evaluated domains.

\end{abstract}

Pretraining-data curation determines how much data a language model sees and
which information remains available for learning. Large-corpus pipelines remove
malformed content, apply rule-based or learned filters, and deduplicate at
exact, fuzzy, or semantic levels
\cite{raffel2020c4,penedo2024fineweb,soldaini2024dolma,li2024datacomp}.
Scalable systems make these operators configurable
\cite{nvidia2026nemo,chen2023datajuicer}, but their final keep/drop output can
hide why each item changed. This is important because an invalid record, a
similarity match, a model judgment, and the loss of a rare content group are
different kinds of evidence and should not authorize the same action.

Evidence-bound curation requires each proposed keep, remove, or restore action
to pass an action-specific rule before it is applied. A similarity search may
propose two items for comparison, but it cannot remove either item; removal
requires a verified redundancy relation and a representative that remains in
the output. Likewise, an uncertain Quality judgment is not a negative label,
and Coverage may reverse a removal when support would disappear but may not
create a new deletion. The evidence is therefore bound to the action it can
justify.

In this paper, we introduce a three-stage framework that enforces this rule.
We use Stage A to check explicit input validity and isolate failures from the
usable pool. In Stage B, we verify redundancy relations and apply an
abstention-aware, four-part Quality assessment. In Stage C, we check the
combined result and restore support that earlier decisions would otherwise
eliminate. We record the input fingerprint, rule version, reason,
and representative or restoration link. Changing the text, evidence provider,
or rule invalidates the earlier record instead of silently reusing it.

We evaluate the enforcement mechanism separately from the usefulness of its
output. Runtime audits test whether unsupported actions are rejected and whether
the output can be reproduced. We then use \emph{natural-budget evaluation}:
the frozen output is trained on every packed token it retains, without adding
sampled data to match the Raw corpus. This preserves the practical consequence of
curation---a smaller dataset produces fewer training updates---while benchmarks
measure the resulting compression--capability trade-off. We report a
three-seed code experiment and a three-seed math transfer.

These distinctions matter when the framework is reused. Detectors, rankers,
retrieval methods, and thresholds may change, but the meaning of an accepted
dataset action should not change silently with them. A usable curation record
must therefore answer four questions: what evidence was observed, which action
that evidence was allowed to trigger, which item or group supported the action,
and whether the same decision can be replayed under the recorded versions. We
treat these questions as runtime requirements rather than post-hoc annotations.

Figure~\ref{fig:authority} provides a visual summary of these stage-specific
rules and the dataset actions they permit.

Our contributions in this paper are summarized as follows.
\begin{itemize}
\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}
\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}
\setlength{\parsep}{0pt}
\item We define an evidence-bound decision model that gives Validity,
Redundancy, Quality, and Coverage different permissions instead of collapsing
their signals into one keep/drop score.
\item We implement an A--B--C runtime that separates explicit isolation,
verified representative-preserving removal, positive Quality selection, and
restore-only Coverage.
\item We bind every accepted action to content and policy identities, a reason
code, and any required representative or restoration link, so that we can
reject stale evidence and replay the final corpus.
\item We evaluate one frozen policy on code and mathematics with three seeds,
natural token budgets, and same-corpus Data-Juicer and NeMo Curator baselines,
revealing both its compression and its domain-dependent downstream effects.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work and Design Gap}
\subsection{Corpus Construction and Quality Selection}
C4, RefinedWeb, FineWeb, and Dolma show how extraction, content rules, and
deduplication shape language-model corpora
\cite{raffel2020c4,penedo2023refinedweb,penedo2024fineweb,soldaini2024dolma}.
DataComp-LM compares recipes under controlled training \cite{li2024datacomp}.
QuRating learns four document ratings for sampling and curriculum construction,
while D4 combines deduplication with embedding-based diversification
\cite{wettig2024qurating,tirumala2023d4}. Yet a one-dimensional classifier may
not predict filtering effects across domains or measure intrinsic quality
\cite{longpre2024pretrainer,naitsaada2025illusion}. We instead govern how such
evidence may change the output: one Core cannot compensate for another, and a
Quality score cannot repair invalid input or remove the last supported member.

\subsection{Curation Systems}
Data-Juicer provides composable operators, recipe exploration, and tracing
\cite{chen2023datajuicer}; NeMo Curator supplies distributed filters and exact,
fuzzy, and semantic deduplication \cite{nvidia2026nemo}; and Oasis combines rule
filters, neural filtering, and corpus assessment \cite{zhou2023oasis}. They are
mainly organized as processing pipelines or recipes. Ours is a complementary
decision-control layer that checks whether different observations are sufficient
to keep, remove, or restore an item and then checks the combined result.
NeMo does not require a top-level domain label; corpus compatibility is instead
expressed through the selected operators and thresholds, including specialized
code filters \cite{nvidia2026nemo,nvidia2026filtering}. Our selector consumes no
declared domain, and the same frozen keep/remove/restore rules govern the reported
code and math runs. The decision process therefore does not require a domain label;
this is not a claim that the same Quality model works equally well in every domain.
Table~\ref{tab:responsibility} compares stated guarantees rather than lists of
operators. The distinction is whether a property remains mandatory when
evidence providers and recipes are replaced.
These approaches are therefore not interchangeable empirical baselines:
QuRating and D4 define learned-rating or prototype-selection objectives, while
Data-Juicer and NeMo Curator expose configurable execution systems. The
same-corpus study includes both systems as executable baselines because one
documented code- and math-compatible recipe from each system could be frozen
and reproduced under the shared training protocol. It does not evaluate the full
design space of QuRating, D4, Data-Juicer, or NeMo Curator.

\subsection{Why a Decision-Control Layer Is Distinct}
An ordered filter pipeline computes a final keep/drop bit, but that bit does
not identify which evidence was sufficient for the action. Evidence-bound curation adds
a policy check before each dataset change and after policies are combined:
similarity may retrieve a comparison, a verified Redundancy relation may remove
a non-representative, and Coverage may restore but not exclude. Each accepted
action is bound to the current text, evidence provider, and policy version,
so changing any of them invalidates the earlier decision. These decision rules
therefore stay explicit even when detectors, rankers, or retrieval systems
are replaced.

\subsection{Deduplication and Failure Patterns}
Deduplication reduces memorization and leakage
\cite{lee2022deduplicating}, and The Stack already applies source-code
near-deduplication \cite{kocetkov2023stack}. MinHash and LSH efficiently retrieve
high-overlap candidates \cite{broder1997resemblance}, but programs can share
imports and structure while differing in behavior. For example,
\texttt{return x >= 0} and \texttt{return x > 0} may collide without either
representing the other. Conversely, uncertain partial fragments can all fail
positive selection without being known incorrect. The rule set therefore
requires relation verification for removal and permits Coverage to restore a
representative when the combined local decisions remove every member of a group.

These cases expose a gap between finding a plausible signal and authorizing a
dataset change. Candidate retrieval is deliberately recall-oriented, whereas
removal needs a verified relation and a named survivor. A positive Quality
judgment can justify keeping an item but cannot prove that a similar item is
interchangeable; similarly, an uncertain judgment is not evidence that the item
is invalid. The proposed runtime therefore records retrieval, verification,
selection, and restoration as different operations. This separation lets the
system reject an otherwise plausible action when its evidence does not carry the
required permission.

\section{Evidence-Bound Decision Model}
\subsection{What Each Core Is Allowed to Do}
Let $D$ be an audited input corpus and $U(D)$ the deterministic training units
created from it. A policy version $\pi$ produces an output
$C_\pi\subseteq U(D)$ and a decision log $T_\pi$. For a unit $x$, an evidence producer emits
$e=(x,h_x,p,v,r)$: unit identity, text hash, producer, version, and result. The
observation remains descriptive until a policy permits a dataset change.

Consider two long functions retrieved as possible near duplicates. MinHash
opens the comparison, but cannot remove either function. Redundancy may remove
one only if an allowed relation is verified and a surviving representative is
named. Quality selects a candidate only when Q2--Q4 provide positive support
and no qualified Q1 failure vetoes that support. If those local decisions would erase the final
member of a declared support group, Coverage restores one representative. The
trace therefore explains both an exclusion and any later restoration.

Each Core has one noninterchangeable permission:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Validity} isolates an explicitly defined input failure.
  \item \textbf{Redundancy} removes only a verified non-representative whose
  representative remains in the output.
  \item \textbf{Quality} positively selects a unit under conjunctive Q2--Q4
  evidence, subject to a qualified Q1 failure veto; missing positive evidence
  is \emph{not selected}, not ``bad.''
  \item \textbf{Coverage} restores support lost by combined proposals; it
  cannot originate an exclusion.
\end{enumerate}

The separation is intentional because the four questions are not different
estimates of one hidden score. A unit can be well formed but redundant, unique
but unsupported by the Quality Ranker, or locally selectable while being the
last member of a support group. Collapsing these cases into one score would let
strong evidence on one axis compensate for a failure on another. It would also
make the same numeric change mean isolation, representative removal, lack of
positive selection, or restoration. The action-specific Core interface instead preserves
both the reason for a decision and the action that reason is allowed to cause.

\subsection{Rules for Dataset Changes}
Every proposal $p(x)$ that changes the corpus must name the current text hash
$h_x$, its evidence $e$, and the versioned policy $\pi$:
\begin{equation}
p(x)\Rightarrow
\mathrm{valid}(\pi,e,h_x)\land\mathrm{permitted}(\pi,e,x).
\label{eq:authority}
\end{equation}
We enforce five testable properties at runtime. Every change names its evidence,
policy, action, and reason. Every Redundancy removal leaves its named
representative in the output. Coverage prevents the unexplained loss of every
member of a support group. Downstream evaluation cannot feed back into
selection. Finally, a rerun rejects reused evidence when the data, provider,
model, policy, or threshold identity has changed.

Let $V$ be Stage B-eligible units, $P_\pi$ the reason-coded non-selection set,
and $\widetilde C_\pi=V\setminus P_\pi$. For every active support group $g$, Stage C
requires a remaining member or a recorded policy exception $a_g$:
\begin{equation}
I_g(\widetilde C_\pi)=
\mathbf{1}[g\cap\widetilde C_\pi\neq\emptyset\ \lor\ a_g=1].
\label{eq:coverage}
\end{equation}
If $I_g=0$, Stage C records the IDs that must be kept, $R_\pi$, forms
$C_\pi=\widetilde C_\pi\cup R_\pi$, and reruns the checks. Missing or stale
evidence rejects the proposed change rather than activating a looser fallback.

The trace distinguishes explicit rejection from absence of positive selection
evidence. Benchmark scores, training loss, utility estimates, source
reputation, target domain shares, retention fractions, and token budgets are
not selector inputs. These rules govern what evidence may do; they do not
claim that every detector is semantically correct.

Table~\ref{tab:unit-trace} summarizes the unit-level contract. An absent unit
must name a closed Validity reason, a surviving Redundancy representative, or
a Quality selection decision; a restored unit names the Coverage group that
required it. If text, evidence, or policy identity changes, a rerun rejects the
inherited decision.

We design the resulting artifact to answer these questions without rerunning a
benchmark or rescoring the corpus. It records not only the curated corpus, but also a
machine-checkable explanation of how that exact corpus was formed.

\subsection{How the Cores Work Together}
We combine the Cores as an ordered sequence of dataset changes, not as majority
voting among four classifiers. Stage A first determines which records and
chunks are eligible for curation. Its isolation decisions are final because
later evidence cannot repair missing or corrupt input. Stage B then creates a
provisional keep set. Explicit evidence of non-training content may exclude a unit;
a verified Redundancy relation may remove only the linked non-representative;
and every remaining unit must satisfy the policy's positive Quality-selection
rule. A unit that lacks enough positive evidence is recorded as not selected,
without being relabeled invalid or intrinsically low quality.

Stage C evaluates the provisional set as a whole. It receives support groups
and the complete reason-coded proposal set, but no permission to create another
exclusion. When Stage B has removed every member of a declared group without a
recorded policy exception, Stage C restores one deterministic member. Commit is
allowed only after all isolated items, surviving representatives, Quality decisions, and
restoration links pass a second hash-and-identity check. Consequently, no Core can
silently reinterpret another Core's evidence: Quality cannot validate corrupt
input, similarity cannot prove redundancy, and Coverage cannot declare a unit
useful or delete it.

We evaluate and package one frozen beta policy rather than multiple strength
profiles. The reported confirmatory runs materialize its Coverage decisions and
pass the complete recheck, but do not claim production promotion. The policy
receives no target corpus size, retention fraction, source prior,
or domain quota. The final size is therefore an outcome of the recorded
evidence rather than an input budget, and changing any rule or threshold creates
a new policy identity rather than silently changing the meaning of the release.

\section{Curation Pipeline}
\subsection{Stage A: Explicit Validity Failures}
The input adapter preserves declared metadata without inferring missing rights
or source history. Stage A applies only content-preserving normalization and
isolates observable input failures: absent text, a declared text-format
violation, unrecoverable corruption, or acquisition failure. Short code,
partial files, unfamiliar formats, markup, multilingual text, and parser
failure remain valid by default. Rights, PII, poisoning, and source-admission
review are separate audit concerns; they are not silently converted into a
text-quality score.

Released records are chunked deterministically into units of at most 6,000 characters.
Every chunk retains its parent record ID and normalized text hash. Stage B
first removes normalized exact duplicates, before candidate retrieval or
model-assisted Quality is considered.

\subsection{Stage B: Verified Redundancy}
Candidate retrieval combines exact text and formatting digests, exact
containment-window digests, and MinHash LSH. The active retrieval settings are
5-token shingles, a 32-value signature, eight bands, and a 24-token retrieval
minimum. The frozen policy considers near-duplicate pairs from 64 tokens with
at most four changed tokens and 2\%; units of at most 32 tokens are exact-only.

Retrieval only schedules comparisons. A removal proposal requires a verified
relation allowed by policy: exact text, formatting equivalence, a near-duplicate
that differs only within declared limits, prose reflow with unchanged tokens,
exact token containment, or a declared equivalence verifier. Numeric, operator,
negation, answer
label, API-signature, code-identifier/keyword, and named-entity changes trigger
substantive-difference protection. Pairwise matches are not chained into larger
duplicate groups. The item that fully contains the other's usable content is
preferred as representative, with a
stable ID tie break.

This split between retrieval and decision is essential to the paper's claim.
The completed run evaluated thousands of candidate pairs, yet every verified
redundancy exclusion was justified by exact token containment rather than
MinHash similarity alone (Section~\ref{sec:materialization}).

\subsection{Stage B: Explicit Non-Training Content Rules}
Four explicit text rules detect a generated-and-do-not-edit artifact,
a license-comment-only chunk, an empty HTML shell, or a chunk containing only
a fixed vocabulary of web controls. Each requires positive in-text evidence.
A generated source label alone, ordinary documentation, code with a license
header, prose about cookies, and HTML with visible or executable content are
protected negative cases. Exact sentences repeated at least three times within
one chunk may be compacted when the sentence contains at least 12 lexical
tokens. The transformation is recorded separately from whole-chunk selection
and never supplies Redundancy or Quality evidence.

\subsection{Stage B: Quality Ranker and Positive Selection}
A scalar Quality score can hide why a unit was selected and can let strength
on one dimension compensate for a known failure on another. We therefore use
four separate, non-compensating judgments; a strong result on one cannot cancel an explicit
failure on another:
\begin{equation}
Q(x)=(Q_1,Q_2,Q_3,Q_4),\qquad Q_i\in\{P,F,A\},
\label{eq:quality}
\end{equation}
where $P$, $F$, and $A$ denote pass, fail, and abstain.
Table~\ref{tab:quality-heads} states the question owned by each head and the
evidence required for an explicit failure. These are specific, locally
testable questions rather than claims of
universal document quality.

The ranker is constructed in three steps. First, GPT-5.6 Luna supplies Q1--Q4
labels under a versioned Batch prompt. Second, four local classifier heads are
fit over frozen Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B representations. Third, minimum confidence
0.8 and an out-of-distribution (OOD) cosine-similarity boundary of 0.3822 mark
unsupported local predictions for fallback instead of forcing a label.
External knowledge, undeclared execution assumptions, missing context, and
unfamiliar notation therefore do not become automatic failures.

Q1 acts only as a qualified-failure veto, while positive membership support uses
the conjunctive payload rule
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{Select}(x)=\mathbf{1}[&Q_1(x)\neq F\land Q_2(x)=P\\
                              &\land Q_3(x)=P\land Q_4(x)=P].
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:selection}
\end{equation}
If any local head abstains, is low-confidence, or is OOD, a frozen Luna
observation applies the same questions. Conjunctive Q2--Q4 fallback support
retains the chunk unless a qualified Q1 failure vetoes it; confirmed required-head
failure or a completed fallback without positive support records it as not selected.
A missing observation or transport failure aborts materialization rather than
changing membership. Thus unverifiable correctness is not treated as failure,
but a locally checkable Q1 failure can veto selection; no weighted aggregate can
compensate for a failed required head.

\subsection{Stage C: Coverage Restoration}
Coverage evaluates the combined Stage B result across overlapping views:
redundancy families, deterministic content routes, language/script and format
labels, residual links, and semantic support groups. The recorded semantic graph
uses Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B as its primary provider and BGE-M3 as an independent
audit view \cite{zhang2025qwen3embedding,chen2024bgem3}. Mutual eight-neighbor
relations shared by both views define stable local support; neighborhoods found
by only one provider are marked uncertain. Embedding similarity never authorizes
an exclusion.

When a provisional output removes every member of a group without a recorded
policy exception, Coverage chooses a deterministic representative, records why
it is required, restores it, and performs a second complete check.
Coverage does not maximize a diversity score, impose a quota, or infer that a
rare group is useful. Its role is narrower: if all members of a declared group
are removed without a recorded policy exception, the output is rejected.

\subsection{Auditable Output}
Each boundary writes unit and parent IDs, text and evidence hashes,
policy/provider versions, decisions, reason codes, and any representative or
restore link. The finalization step validates these identities, applies the
A--B--C stages, reruns all decision checks after Coverage, and hashes the
final corpus. Reports aggregate token and chunk impact by reason and use
comparable Stage B-to-C denominators for composition changes.

Interrupted runs resume only when every bound identity matches. A stale or
corrupt cache reduces usable evidence or aborts output construction; it cannot
activate a permissive fallback. The output corpus and its explanation are thus
versioned together.

\section{Implementation}
\subsection{Hash-Checked Execution}
We separate policy specification, evidence artifacts, and
final output generation. JSON configuration files bind the source manifest, tokenizer, chunking,
model and provider identities, the active policy, and output locations. A
runtime bridge rejects a resume if a bound identity or content hash changes.
The release manifest binds one active policy and rejects legacy profile names.
Table~\ref{tab:implementation} lists the versioned settings that materially
affect the final corpus. Luna is an offline label and frozen fallback-evidence
source, and Qwen3-4B-Base is
used only for external training; neither is an undeclared selector feature.

The execution graph is partitionable at corpus scale. Stage A checks,
per-unit Quality inference, and pairwise relation verification are keyed by
stable unit or pair identities and require no shared mutable state. Exact
digests and MinHash signatures can be built per shard before a keyed merge;
Stage C is the only corpus-wide check, aggregating declared support
groups before deterministic output construction. Decision traces are append-only
and keyed by the same identities. This structure makes the decision layer
compatible with distributed curation engines, although the present study does
not report distributed throughput or storage benchmarks.

\subsection{Safe Output Construction}
We build the output in one all-or-nothing step: if any required check fails,
no final output is written. The steps are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item validate the source, tokenizer, schema, policy, and model identities;
  \item keep or isolate records under Stage A and create deterministic
  chunk identities;
  \item retrieve Redundancy candidates, verify allowed relations, apply closed
  non-training-content rules, and obtain version-bound Q1--Q4 evidence;
  \item write the provisional keep set together with every decision and
  representative link;
  \item run all Coverage views, restore required representatives, and repeat
  the complete decision check; and
  \item hash the final corpus before exposing it to external training or
  evaluation.
\end{enumerate}
An interrupted run resumes only when all bound identities still match. Missing
or stale evidence reduces the set of permitted actions or aborts the run; it
cannot activate a looser fallback. This lifecycle makes the curated corpus and
its explanation one versioned artifact.

\subsection{User-Facing Inputs and Outputs}
A deployment begins with JSONL records containing text and stable source-local
identifiers; optional metadata is preserved but is not required to name a
domain. A run configuration binds the frozen framework policy, tokenizer,
normalization, frozen Quality Ranker, semantic providers, active policy files,
and output location. Automatic mode computes the required embeddings and
Coverage graph from all Stage B-eligible units. A caller may instead supply frozen
artifacts with matching identities, but precomputed and automatically generated
evidence cannot be mixed within one run.

The returned object is an audit bundle rather than only a filtered JSONL file.
It contains normalized input, Stage A isolated and pass sets, Stage B
selection and non-selection proposals, the final curated and not-selected
corpora after Stage C, Redundancy representative links, Coverage restoration
traces, per-reason token and chunk deltas, descriptive route and script
composition reports, and a manifest of policy, provider, input, and output
hashes. Thus a user can ask which evidence changed a unit's keep/remove status, which
representative justified a removal, whether Coverage reversed the proposal, and
whether a later run used the same decision boundary. Composition reports remain
descriptive and never force a target mixture.

\subsection{Scope of the Quality Model}
The distilled ranker is a replaceable, versioned policy component rather than
a universal quality classifier. Its encoder identity, four heads, OOD boundary,
confidence thresholds, and protected-set checks are bound to the output trace.
Replacing the teacher, encoder, labels, or curation policy invalidates that
calibration and requires a new policy version; it cannot silently alter an
existing corpus.

\subsection{Implementation Checks}
Positive, false-positive, adversarial, and input-variation tests exercise each
permitted action. Corpus reports check action-specific Quality decisions,
representative survival, restore-only Stage C, and the complete post-Coverage
recheck. Source, leakage, and packed-input
reports bind hashes and exact token, block, and step counts. These checks show
that the implementation follows the stated rules; they do not establish semantic
accuracy or downstream benefit.

\section{Experimental Setup}
We design the experiments to test two distinct questions. Runtime evidence checks whether
the implementation allows only permitted dataset changes; continued pretraining and
benchmarks characterize the completed corpora after their contents are fixed.

\subsection{Corpora and Benchmark-Overlap Checks}
The code Raw arm contains 4,228 records from
\texttt{bigcode/the-stack-dedup} and 662 records from pinned snapshots of eight
open-source repositories. A pre-curation audit removes 12 exact or
16-token-shingle benchmark matches. This establishes
the declared text-matching boundary, not absence of semantic or base-model
contamination. Quality calibration and protected samples are UID- and
text-hash-disjoint. The full run uses the fixed local ranker and frozen Luna
fallback observations, without live teacher calls, benchmark, utility,
source-score, or token-budget inputs.

The exploratory math arm is a separately sampled OpenWebMath corpus
\cite{paster2023openwebmath}. A pre-curation audit removes one full-MATH
containment match from 3,232 records and finds no remaining MATH-500 match under
the declared text-matching test \cite{cobbe2021gsm8k,hendrycks2021math}. The same
frozen framework policy is applied to code and math without a declared domain
label or domain-specific retuning.

For direct external comparisons, the audited code and math inputs are also
processed by frozen Data-Juicer and NeMo Curator 1.3.0 recipes. The Data-Juicer
code recipe adapts the published Stack refinement recipe: normalization and
cleanup precede length, repetition, and alphanumeric filters and document-level
SimHash deduplication. Its math recipe similarly adapts the published arXiv
refinement recipe, retaining math-compatible character, length, repetition,
and perplexity filters followed by SimHash deduplication
\cite{chen2023datajuicer}. The NeMo code recipe applies the
documented Python comment-to-code, line-count, alphabetic-content, and
XML-header filters. The math-compatible recipe uses length, boilerplate,
repeated-line, repeated-paragraph, and repeating-$n$-gram filters while
excluding text heuristics that would penalize mathematical notation. Both then
apply MD5 exact and MinHash-LSH fuzzy deduplication
\cite{nvidia2026nemo,nvidia2026filtering}; semantic deduplication and target
token budgets are disabled. Each system arm represents one declared recipe,
not the full configuration space of either system.

\subsection{Training and Evaluation Protocol}
We continue Qwen3-4B-Base \cite{yang2025qwen3} on audited Raw, the final
framework output, Data-Juicer, and NeMo outputs.
Each arm uses its complete natural token stream; there is no equal-token
resampling, target retention fraction, or maximum-token cap. The final framework
code experiment and the math transfer use seeds 101, 202, and 303. All arms
share the same model revision, tokenizer, one-pass
packing, sequence length 2,048, QLoRA configuration, and optimization recipe;
only the selected data and resulting number of steps differ. We record the
complete training, decoding, task-snapshot, and evaluator identities by
hash in the reproducibility package.
The hash-checked artifact is available at
\url{https://github.com/LeeKeonSoo/UNLV-Research/blob/main/paper/Evidence_Bound_Curation_Reproducibility.zip}.

We report deterministic pass@1 separately for HumanEval+, MBPP+,
BigCodeBench Complete, CRUXEval-I/O, and DS-1000; every arm, seed, and task is
retained rather than collapsed into a single aggregate score
\cite{liu2023evalplus,zhuo2025bigcodebench,gu2024cruxeval,lai2023ds1000}.

We reuse the same recipe in the math experiment and evaluate Raw, the final
framework output, Data-Juicer, and NeMo Curator with seeds 101, 202, and 303 on
frozen GSM8K and MATH-500 snapshots. Math remains an exploratory transfer
rather than a confirmatory domain-general endpoint.

GSM8K strict and flexible extraction can disagree when a response continues
into another generated problem. We retain both official scores and report one
identically applied normalized rescore as a post-hoc sensitivity audit; it does
not replace either official endpoint.

\section{Results}
We first report the frozen corpora and decision path. Because Raw units are
records and curated units are chunks, corpus comparisons use tokenizer counts
rather than unit counts. The code corpus changes from 6,984,438 to 6,249,197
tokenizer tokens (89.47\% retained), and math changes from 6,984,004 to
5,780,762 (82.77\% retained). Complete optimizer groups contain 6,242,304 and
5,767,168 training tokens, respectively.

\subsection{Composition Effects}
Source identity and target domain shares are not selector inputs. The output
bundle nevertheless reports content route, document format, language/script,
and document-function shares before and after curation. These deterministic
labels explain composition shifts; they are neither semantic ground truth nor
target proportions and never force a quota.

\subsection{Code Results}
We completed the three-seed framework evaluation under the frozen protocol.
HumanEval+ is complete at 31.50\%, compared with 31.10\% for Base and 28.86\%
for Raw. MBPP+ reaches 54.67\%, below Base (58.47\%) but above Raw (48.94\%),
Data-Juicer (41.27\%), and NeMo Curator (50.97\%). CRUXEval-I/O reach
5.79\%/2.83\%; the input score trails Raw and both curation baselines, whereas
the output score exceeds all four references. BigCodeBench reaches 39.12\%,
below all four references, whereas DS-1000 reaches 34.13\%, above Base and Raw
but below Data-Juicer and NeMo Curator. Overall, the framework exceeds both
same-corpus curation baselines on three of six Code benchmarks and exceeds Base
on four of six.

\subsection{Exploratory Math Results}
The same frozen policy retains 82.77\% of the math tokenizer stream without a
domain label or math-specific threshold. Across three seeds, it scores 77.53\%
on GSM8K strict, 63.08\% on GSM8K flexible, 79.05\% under the normalized
sensitivity rescore, and 7.07\% on MATH-500. All four values exceed Base but
trail Raw and both executable curation baselines. This transfer shows that the
decision process executes consistently outside code, while the downstream
result does not establish domain-general effectiveness.

\section{Discussion}
\subsection{What the Runtime Establishes}
Runtime audits and downstream training answer different questions. The former
show that retrieval alone caused no deletion, all verified removals retained a
representative, and 104/143 Coverage restores prevented the unsupported loss of
every item in a Code/Math support group.
The latter evaluates each completed corpus under its natural budget. A
benchmark gain cannot validate every local decision, and a regression does not
invalidate the trace; it prevents us from claiming that the frozen policy is effective.

\subsection{Tracing the Mixed Results}
We use the decision log to identify the dominant intervention. Explicit structural
rules exclude 40/1 chunks, normalized exact deduplication removes 1/4, and
verified Redundancy removes 3/6 in code/math,
whereas Quality records 816/1,223 not-selected chunks before Coverage restores
104/143. Repeated-sentence compaction transforms 222/443 chunks without
changing their membership. Coverage prevents complete group loss, not utility
loss within a surviving group, so the trace supports diagnosis without claiming
a causal mechanism.
The local heads resolve 1,807/7,982 Code decisions but only 48/6,608 Math
decisions; frozen Luna fallback observations supply the remaining
77.36\%/99.27\%. The Math transfer therefore primarily evaluates the frozen
teacher-backed fallback rather than autonomous local-ranker transfer.

\subsection{Relationship to Existing Systems}
QuRating strengthens evidence production, while NeMo Curator and Data-Juicer
provide operator libraries and scalable execution. Evidence-bound curation is
a complementary decision layer: retrieval proposes, a verified relation may
remove, positive Quality evidence may select, and Coverage may only restore.
Hash and identity checks enforce these limits while allowing the ranker, retriever,
support graph, or execution engine to be replaced.

\subsection{Interpreting the Frozen Comparison}
The completed comparison characterizes the compression--capability trade-off of
the single framework output relative to Raw and the two executable systems.
Compression alone does not establish success, and the task-level outcomes remain
mixed. Our distinct claim is the reproducible decision path;
downstream scores bound how strongly the particular frozen policy can be
recommended.

\section{Limitations}
We complement existing curation operators rather than claiming that general
curation systems are absent. Our contribution is the enforceable and
inspectable connection between different forms of evidence and allowed dataset
changes; the effectiveness of any frozen policy still requires empirical
evaluation.

The evaluation compares complete curation systems, not rule-level causal
effects. Raw's Stack component was already deduplicated, limiting observable
Redundancy removal, and retention may reflect length, style, familiarity, or
usable content. Normalized GSM8K is a post-hoc sensitivity audit. Quality
calibration uses synthetic fixtures and
teacher labels; the stored observations are versioned and reusable, but they
cannot be independently regenerated without the same proprietary teacher.
Coverage graphs define reproducible support candidates rather than semantic
ground truth. Their reported materializations and complete rechecks are limited
to the confirmatory code and math runs and do not constitute production
promotion. Linking available repository rights metadata to every record also
remains incomplete for a self-contained corpus release.

We evaluate at a smaller scale than production pretraining: two roughly 6.98M-token
corpora and QLoRA continued pretraining. The final code and trained math arms
have three seeds. Distributed throughput, wall time, peak memory, and
energy are not benchmarked. General-text and multilingual effects remain
unevaluated, and each external-system comparison covers one frozen recipe. The
keep/remove/restore rules are evaluated only under the reported corpora and training protocol.
The method-level approaches in
Table~\ref{tab:responsibility} are not same-corpus trained baselines. Claims therefore
apply to the reported corpora, policies, model, recipes, and scorers rather than
universal downstream effectiveness.

\section{Conclusion}
Evidence-bound curation makes keep, remove, and restore decisions auditable by
giving Validity, Redundancy, Quality, and Coverage separate actions. In the
final code and math runs, similarity retrieval alone never removed data, every
Redundancy removal named a surviving representative, and Coverage restored
support before output materialization. The same single policy retains 89.47\%
of code and 82.77\% of math tokenizer tokens and passes the complete runtime
recheck without using target size or downstream utility.

The completed evaluations show mixed downstream effects: the frozen policy
exceeds both curation baselines on three of six Code benchmarks but trails them
on all four reported Math scores. The frozen beta decision layer does not require a
declared domain and keeps each corpus change tied to reproducible evidence. The
empirical claim therefore remains limited to the completed code and math
protocols.
